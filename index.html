HR Analytics

End-to-end HR analytics case study — Python · SQL · Power BI · Excel PythonPandasSQLPower BIExcel

Business problem

Acme Corporation (a fictional case-study company; the data is the public IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition & Performance dataset) is losing employees at 16.1% — well above the 10% benchmark management would target. Replacing an employee costs hiring fees, onboarding time and institutional knowledgeAcme Corporation (a fictional case-study company; the data is the public IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition & Performance dataset) is losing employees at 16.1% — well above the 10% benchmark management would target. Replacing an employee costs hiring fees, onboarding time and institutional knowledge.

Key insights

  • Attrition is 16.12% (237 of 1,470) — above the 10% company benchmark used in this analysis, and above the rate a mature employer would typically target.
  • There is no evidence that high performers (rating 4) leave disproportionately (16.4% vs 16.1% for rating 3) — visible top-talent hemorrhage is not what this data shows.
  • Low job satisfaction is strongly associated with attrition (22.8% at score 1 vs 11.3% at score 4), as is lack of training (27.8% at 0 courses vs 9.2% at 6).
  • The direct payroll of leavers was ≈ $1.13M/month (≈$13.6M/yr); replacement costs (not in the data) would add to that.

Results

  • $2,296,919 revenue · $286,409 profit · 12.47% margin · 5,009 orders — every metric cross-validated in Python and SQL.
  • 65 SQL queries validated against pandas, 12 business-driven charts, and a 3-page Power BI dashboard with synced slicers.
  • Executive report mapping each insight to a concrete recommendation (discount governance, SKU pruning, regional turnaround).

02

Customer Sales Dashboard

End-to-end customer analytics on 1.07 million real e-commerce transactions: from rawinvoice lines to RFM segments, a four-page BI dashboard, and a prioritised set ofmarketing actions. PythonNumpyPower BIclass="tag">jupyter

Business problem

A UK-based online giftware retailer sells to a mix of consumers and wholesale buyers.Management can see total revenue, but cannot see who is producing it. Marketing spend isallocated evenly across the customer base because there is no evidence for doing anythingelse.

Objectives

  • Turn raw transaction lines into a trustworthy, documented analytical dataset.
  • Establish the commercial baseline — customers, orders, revenue, order value, frequency.
  • Score every customer on Recency, Frequency and Monetary value.

Key insights

  • Revenue is extremely concentrated. The top 10% of customers generate 63.2% ofnet revenue; the top 1% generate 31.1%; just 272 customers (4.6%) account for halfof all revenue. Gini coefficient 0.737.
  • Champions carry the business. 1,282 customers (21.9%) produce 69.3% of revenue,averaging £8,839 each across 17.0 orders. They are the only segment whose revenue sharematerially exceeds its headcount share (efficiency index 3.16).
