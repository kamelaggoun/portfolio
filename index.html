03

Sales Forecasting & Time Series Analysis

Retail sales forecasting with chronological validation — and an honest error rate attached to it. Python Pandas SQL statsmodels Matplotlib Excel Power BI 1,033,036 cleaned records ~12% monthly MAPE Chronological validation
Business problem

Retail businesses need reliable sales forecasts for planning inventory, staffing, purchasing, and revenue expectations. This project analyses more than one million retail transaction records and builds a time-series forecasting workflow from historical sales patterns.

Dataset

UCI Online Retail II — 1,033,036 cleaned transaction lines after preparation and exclusions.

Analysis
  • Cleaned and prepared large-scale retail transaction data, aggregating transactions into a chronological sales series.
  • Evaluated forecasting approaches using chronological (time-based) validation rather than random train-test splitting.
  • Measured forecast performance with monthly MAPE as the error metric.
  • Documented resulting KPIs and dashboard requirements for Power BI and Excel reporting.
Key findings
  • A seasonal-naive forecasting approach achieved approximately 12% monthly MAPE for the analysed forecasting setup.
  • Random train-test splitting gives an unrealistic view of forecast accuracy on time-series data; chronological validation is required.
  • Cleaning decisions matter at this scale: the workflow reduces the raw transactional data to 1,033,036 analysable lines before the series is built.
Business value

Gives planning teams a defensible baseline forecast with a measured error level attached, so inventory, staffing, and purchasing decisions are made against a stated expectation rather than an assumption.

02

Customer Segmentation

RFM segmentation on 1.07 million transaction lines — which customers actually carry the revenue. Python Pandas SQL Power BI 1.07M transaction lines Top 10% = 63.2% of revenue Gini 0.737 concentration
Business problem

A UK-based online giftware retailer serves a mix of consumers and wholesale buyers. Management can see total revenue but not which customers generate it. The analysis segments customers by value and behaviour to support targeted marketing and retention decisions.

Dataset

1.07 million transaction lines from a UK-based online giftware retailer, covering both consumer and wholesale buyers.

Analysis
  • Cleaned and prepared more than one million transaction lines for analytical use.
  • Defined commercial KPIs: customers, orders, revenue, average order value, and purchase frequency.
  • Applied Recency, Frequency, Monetary (RFM) scoring to classify customers into segments.
  • Used SQL to validate analytical calculations and Power BI to communicate the resulting segments.
Key findings
  • The top 10% of customers generate 63.2% of net revenue; the top 1% generate 31.1%.
  • 272 customers — 4.6% of the customer base — account for half of total revenue.
  • The revenue distribution has a Gini coefficient of 0.737, confirming substantial concentration.
  • Champions make up 21.9% of customers but generate 69.3% of revenue, making them the highest-priority segment for retention.
Business value

Shows where retention and marketing effort returns the most: a small group of customers carries the majority of revenue, and the segments to target first are named and measurable.

HR Analytics

Employee attrition analysis — which workforce factors are associated with people leaving. Python Pandas SQL Power BI Excel 1,470 employee records 16.12% attrition 65 SQL queries cross-validated
Business problem

HR management can see headcount and total turnover, but not which workforce factors are associated with employees leaving. This analysis investigates attrition and converts the findings into practical retention priorities.

Dataset

IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition & Performance — a public dataset of 1,470 employee records covering satisfaction, training, performance, income, and tenure, analysed as a fictional case-study company.

Analysis
  • Data cleaning and consistency checks across 1,470 employee records.
  • Exploratory analysis of attrition against satisfaction, training, performance, and income.
  • 65 SQL queries cross-validated against Python and Pandas calculations.
  • KPI analysis and a 3-page Power BI report with interactive filters and synchronised slicers.
Key findings
  • Overall attrition is 16.12% — 237 employees out of 1,470 in the dataset.
  • Attrition is 22.8% for employees with a job-satisfaction score of 1, compared with 11.3% at a score of 4.
  • Attrition is 27.8% among employees with no training courses, compared with 9.2% among those who completed 6.
  • Correlation is kept separate from unsupported assumptions, so findings are presented as associations rather than proven causes.
Business value

Gives HR a prioritised view of where attrition risk concentrates — satisfaction and training exposure — so retention effort can be targeted instead of applied as a blanket policy.

SQL Business Analysis

Advanced SQL on a relational business database — from data quality checks to cohort and LTV analysis. SQL Python Pandas Power BI 10 documented SQL scripts CTEs · window functions Cohorts · LTV · Pareto
Business problem

Business data often contains quality issues and requires more than simple aggregation to answer commercial questions. This project moves from raw relational data to validated business analysis using advanced SQL.

Dataset

Relational business database covering customers, orders, order items, products, and regions, analysed through 10 documented SQL scripts — from data quality through to advanced analytics.

Analysis
  • Audited data quality and validated analytical assumptions before reporting.
  • Multi-CTE queries with NTILE window functions to build RFM scores directly in SQL.
  • Sequential LAG/LEAD analysis, recursive CTE calendar series, and rolling 3-month moving averages.
  • Churn detection using subqueries and EXISTS, market-basket affinity pairs, and matrix pivoting.
  • Cross-checked SQL outputs against Python and Pandas calculations.
Key findings
  • Advanced SQL constructs are applied to real business questions: cohort retention, customer lifetime value, revenue concentration, and churn.
  • Cohort and Pareto analysis identify how much of the business depends on a small share of customers.
  • SQL results are cross-validated against Python and Pandas before they are used for reporting.
  • Results are translated into a Power BI reporting structure suitable for business stakeholders.
Business value

Demonstrates the database work employers test directly: analytical SQL that is readable, validated, and reusable — not just single-table aggregations.

SQL excerpt — sql/10_advanced_sql.sql
WITH customer_rfm_raw AS (
    SELECT
        c.customer_id,
        (CAST('2024-12-31' AS DATE) - MAX(CAST(o.order_date AS DATE))) AS recency_days,
        COUNT(DISTINCT o.order_id) AS frequency_orders,
        SUM(oi.line_total)         AS monetary_spend
    FROM customers c
    JOIN orders o       ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id
    JOIN order_items oi ON o.order_id    = oi.order_id
    WHERE o.order_status NOT IN ('Cancelled', 'Returned')
    GROUP BY c.customer_id
),
scored_rfm AS (
    SELECT
        customer_id,
        NTILE(4) OVER (ORDER BY recency_days DESC)    AS r_tile,
        NTILE(4) OVER (ORDER BY frequency_orders ASC) AS f_tile,
        NTILE(4) OVER (ORDER BY monetary_spend ASC)   AS m_tile
    FROM customer_rfm_raw
)
SELECT * FROM scored_rfm;
